# Learning expectations

Help the user choose the next teachable skills without turning one source's age or grade labels into a judgment about a child.

## Establish the context

Determine the child's age at the relevant date, grade or program, jurisdiction, learning setting, language, and observed behavior in the requested domains. Ask only for missing details that would change the advice; otherwise state an assumption. Record what the child can do, how independently, and with what materials or prompts. Distinguish recitation, recognition, explanation, and transfer.

## Research expectations

Use WebSearch and WebFetch for current primary sources when available. Classify every expectation before using it:

| Type | Typical source | Proper use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal or program requirement | Current jurisdiction or program text | Compliance only when applicable; verify date and enrollment path. |
| Developmental milestone or progression | CDC, Head Start, original developmental research | Describe common development and variation; not a pass/fail curriculum. |
| End-of-grade standard | Current state education agency | Identify an instructional destination for that grade, not a prerequisite for entering it. |
| Curriculum content guideline | Core Knowledge Sequence and grade-level “What Your … Needs to Know” books | Choose coherent knowledge and experiences; do not present as universal developmental milestones. |
| Evidence-based teaching recommendation | IES practice guide or primary study | Choose instructional methods; do not infer that a named commercial product is validated. |

For preschool, consider the current Head Start ELOF, state early learning standards, and the 2023 Core Knowledge Preschool Sequence. For K–8 content, consider the current Core Knowledge Sequence and the relevant *What Your … Needs to Know* volume. Verify edition and actual contents from the publisher or book before citing. The Core Knowledge books are curated curriculum references; they do not establish what every child must already know. Keep curriculum content knowledge distinct from independent reading or writing ability: a child may learn sophisticated topics through read-alouds.

If sources disagree, show the age band, definition, and purpose of each source. Do not average them into an invented norm. Avoid generalizations from sex, a single test score, or an isolated skill. Do not diagnose. If there is persistent loss of skills or a substantial concern, direct the family to an appropriate professional without using the research table as a screening test.

## Turn sources into a plan

For each domain, give a concise map: sourced reference point and its type; child's current demonstration; the next prerequisite or stretch; an observable activity that would show progress. Choose targets based on demonstrated capability and family goals, not the book's title. Suggest dated work samples or brief recordings when longitudinal comparison would help.

Link directly to the supporting source near each important claim. Mark the date for standards, prices, and program rules that may change. If the user's project already has an expectations guide, update that authoritative guide instead of making a second competing one.
